\section{Methodology}
\label{3-sec:3}

This chapter sets out the methodology that produces the business-economic / socio-economic gap quantification at the centre of the thesis. The design is deliberately compact: a single-case study \autoref{3-subsec:3.1 research design} executed through four analytical steps \autoref{3-subsec:3.2 analytical approach}. A technical flexibility envelope sizes the physical resource first, the business-economic model \autoref{3-subsec:3.3 DH operator model} and the socio-economic triangulation \autoref{3-subsec:3.4 socio-economic valuation} carry the quantitative weight, and the qualitative interviews \autoref{3-subsec:3.5 interviews} reinforce mechanism attribution. \autoref{3-subsec:3.6} sets out the validity safeguards — construct, internal, external, and reliability checks — alongside an Atabaki-style coverage-hygiene check; \autoref{3-subsec:3.7} names the method-level limitations the design carries by construction. A systematic review of 123 district-heating benefit-assessment studies found that flexibility, as a benefit category, has been identified eleven times, quantified four times, and monetized zero times across the corpus \cite{ahmed_district_2025}. The absence of a harmonized method is therefore not a gap this thesis closes by importing an off-the-shelf framework, but the warrant for building a small bespoke framework defended layer by layer.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Research Design}
\label{3-subsec:3.1 research design}

The research design is a single-case study with embedded sub-units, a design chosen because the question requires both depth on one regulatory-and-operator system and analytical generalization to a class of similar systems.

The case is Oslo's district-heating system as a system, not Hafslund Celsio as an organization. The unit of analysis includes regulatory context (RME, Statnett, NVE concessions, energiloven section 5-5), municipal planning, the transmission and distribution grids that bound the operator's flexibility envelope, and the load-side relationships that shape demand. Hafslund Celsio is the operator at the center of the case, but the case is the system the operator dispatches into. 

The design is single-case with four embedded sub-units. Heat production sources cover installed capacity, fuel mix, and operational flexibility across the waste-to-energy base-load, the heat-pump and electric-boiler peaking stack, and the Haraldrud accumulator. Distribution infrastructure covers network characteristics and temperature regimes. Demand-side relationships cover customer segments and load patterns. Governance arrangements cover ownership, pricing, and planning. Each sub-unit is examined for what it contributes to the BØ/SØ gap mechanism, not as an analytical end in itself.

A multi-city design covering Oslo, Trondheim, and Bergen was considered and rejected. The reduction to a single primary case rests on three grounds. First, the depth-versus-breadth trade-off is decisive at the modeling resolution required: an hourly LP dispatch model calibrated to plant-level capacity, tariff schedules, and operational data cannot be replicated across three operators within the thesis's time envelope without sacrificing the calibration that gives the model its analytical value. Second, the BØ/SØ gap is partly a function of national regulatory context (effektledd, energiloven §5-5, NVE concession terms, Statnett market-design rules) which is uniform across Norwegian cases, and partly a function of operator-specific cost structure which is local; the national-regime layer is captured equally well by any single primary case. Third, Oslo is the best-evidenced Norwegian case in the existing literature. A tariff-binding dispatch model has been calibrated on actual 2012 Hafslund Varme hourly heat-load data and Hafslund Nett tariff rates \cite{kirkerud_impacts_2016}, making Oslo the closest geographic precedent in the corpus for the type of analysis the thesis performs (data-vintage caveat: 2012, but the institutional structure has carried).

The single-case scope is further defensible because Oslo is where the grid constraint bites hardest. At NO1 maximum load, local production covers approximately $10\%$ of demand; Statnett and Elvia have around 1,000 MW of new-load applications with only about 200 MW reservable \cite{Thema_fjernvarme_2024}. The same source values the grid capacity freed by converting Oslo electric heating to waterborne heat and district heating, a potential Celsio has mapped at 1.5–1.9 TWh, at NOK 250–430 m/yr in avoided grid investment and faster connection of queued load. Flexibility is additional to that figure rather than its source: the same report notes that greater use of heat storage and optimized electric-boiler operation can free further capacity on top of the conversion effect, so the SØ side of the gap is anchored on that increment, not on the conversion value itself. Oslo's district-heating system serves roughly $20\%$ of the city's heating demand, and district heating could cover about $25\%$ of the city's capacity demand \cite{energikommisjonen_mer_2023}, making the case both representative of Norwegian DH-as-flexibility-resource potential and bounded enough for tractable analysis.

Trondheim and Bergen remain in the thesis as comparative context, not as primary cases. Trondheim has a smaller DH system with different ownership and a different P2H balance; Bergen sits in NO5 with a different bidding-zone exposure and a smaller flexibility envelope. Each is referenced where Nordic and Norwegian intra-country variation sharpens an argument about the Oslo case, not as a stand-alone unit of analysis.

\subsection{Four-Step Analytical Approach}
\label{3-subsec:3.2 analytical approach}

The methodology unfolds in four steps: a technical flexibility envelope quantifies the physical resource and bounds everything downstream; a business-economic LP dispatch model produces the operator-side ledger; a socio-economic triangulation produces the society-side estimate; the gap arithmetic and barrier attribution compute the difference and map it to the mechanisms that drive it. \autoref{3-subsec:3.3 DH operator model}, \autoref{3-subsec:3.4 socio-economic valuation}, and \autoref{4-subsec:4.3 valuation gap} / \autoref{5-subsec:5.3 implications} carry the full detail; this section is a compressed spine that the reader can recite in four paragraphs.

Step one is the technical flexibility envelope. A first-order engineering calculation establishes how much flexibility physically exists in the Oslo system before any economics enter, in the two dimensions the power system remunerates separately: energy (MWh that can be stored and time-shifted) and power (MW that can be added or shed at short notice). The energy side sums the dispatchable thermal storage stock: the Haraldrud accumulator plus the heat parked in the distribution network's water mass, the latter estimated from network water volume, an admissible operating temperature band, and the specific heat capacity of water. The power side quantifies the headroom for overcapacity injection: the electric load the P2H fleet can draw above concurrent heat demand for a bounded period, limited by installed electric-boiler and heat-pump capacity. Regulation-ability parameters for that headroom, ramp shares per 30 seconds, minimum loads, and start times, are taken from the Danish Energy Agency technology catalogue \cite{danish_energy_agency_technology_2024}. Repeating the same arithmetic under current operating practice gives the share of the envelope the operator utilizes today, so a single pass yields both the technical upper bound and a utilization baseline. The network-as-storage component follows the short-term-storage framing of \cite{hennessy_flexibility_2019} and the network flexibility-unlocking logic of \cite{vandermeulen_controlling_2018}.

The envelope is a static stock-and-headroom estimate, not a dispatch simulation: it quantifies what exists, while the LP in \autoref{3-subsec:3.3 DH operator model} determines what current price signals make worth dispatching. The distinction keeps the envelope consistent with assumption A7 in \autoref{3-subsec:3.3.3 assumptions}, which excludes network thermal dynamics from the optimization. Inputs come from the system description in \autoref{2-subsec:2.4.1 system characteristics} and the data catalogue in \autoref{3-subsec:3.3.2 input data sources}, with operator-confirmed values preferred where Hafslund Celsio grants them. Results are reported as the opening result of \autoref{4-sec:4 results}, where the envelope supplies the physical denominator for the gap arithmetic: the business-economic and socio-economic values in the later steps are expressed against the same MWh and MW base, so the two perspectives stay commensurable.

Step two is the business-economic analysis. A deterministic linear program implemented in Python with PuLP and the open-source CBC solver dispatches Hafslund Celsio's flexible assets (electric boilers, heat pumps, accumulator tank) over a representative year at hourly resolution to minimize net electricity cost net of spot-arbitrage revenue and mFRR participation revenue. The model produces annual operator cashflow and levelized cost of heat (NOK/MWh) under a scenario stack that crosses two tariff regimes (the interruptible-terms baseline and a standard-tariff counterfactual) with three reserve-access ceilings and the two representative years, benchmarked against a no-flexibility counterfactual. The architecture descends from a Nordic deterministic-dispatch lineage \cite{kirkerud_impacts_2016}, \cite{johannsen_incentivising_2021}, \cite{tromborg_flexible_2017}. The full method detail, and the defended step-down from Kirkerud's MILP unit-commitment formulation to the thesis's LP, appear in \autoref{3-subsec:3.3 DH operator model}.

Step three is the socio-economic triangulation. The thesis does not construct a from-scratch cost-benefit analysis. A systematic review of 123 studies identified 26 monetized DH-benefit instances across six methods and found no harmonized framework \cite{ahmed_district_2025}. Flexibility specifically has never been monetized in the corpus. The thesis therefore triangulates published socio-economic values against the THEMA (2024) anchor estimate of NOK 1–1.6 bn/year, isolating the flexibility-attributable subset from THEMA's broader conversion-value headline. Triangulating sources include NVE LA23, NVE (2024) Utviklingen i kraftmarkedet mot 2050, Vista Analyse (2023), SINTEF ZEN Report 47 (Kauko 2023 / 2025), and Grønli/AFRY (2025) at plant level, with corroboration from NOU 2023:3 and the THEMA \& Multiconsult report for OED (2022). The anchor estimate carries a sponsor-bias caveat — THEMA was commissioned by Hafslund Celsio — which is acknowledged once in Section 3.4 and mitigated by triangulation across independent sources.

Step four is the gap analysis and barrier attribution. The arithmetic computes the difference between the operator-side ledger (Section 3.3) and the triangulated society-side estimate (Section 3.4) in NOK/MWh-flex, NOK/MW/year, and NOK/year. Attribution maps the gap onto the four barrier types that organise \autoref{2-subsec:2.3 barriers} — technical, economic and market, regulatory and policy, and institutional — which are this thesis's collapse of the nine categories in the district-energy barrier taxonomy of \cite{sneum_barriers_2021}, mapped category by category in that section. The attribution assigns back-of-envelope NOK shares where the data supports it and qualitative reasoning where it does not. In the resource-and-enabler terms of Sections 2.1.1 and 2.3, each of these barrier categories is a missing or adverse enabler, so the attributed gap measures the cost of the enablers the Norwegian regime withholds from a flexibility resource that physically exists. The theoretical framing that connects the technical gap to the institutional gap comes from the heat-electricity market-coordination literature \cite{mitridati_heat_2020}: district heating functions as virtual electricity storage without a market product to sell that flexibility into. The formal storage-equivalence quantification — a stylized 3.51\% cost reduction and 1.65\% wind uplift on a 6-bus / 3-node case — sits with \cite{mitridati_power_2018}, which the thesis cites for the formal result with the magnitudes-caveat. The detail of the attribution and the policy implications live in \autoref{4-subsec:4.3 valuation gap} and \autoref{5-subsec:5.3 implications}.

\subsubsection{Model Formulation}
\label{3-subsec:3.3.1 model formulation}

The model is a deterministic linear program implemented in Python with the open-source PuLP modeling library and the COIN-OR CBC solver. Horizon is one representative year at hourly resolution. The choice of LP rather than mixed-integer linear programming is a deliberate step-down from \cite{kirkerud_impacts_2016}, the closest Norwegian methodological precedent, which is a MILP unit-commitment formulation calibrated on Hafslund Varme 2012 data. Celsio's flexible assets — electric boilers, large heat pumps, accumulator tank — have no binding start-up or minimum-load constraints at the operator scale relevant to the thesis's question, so dropping the unit-commitment binaries removes machinery the model does not need without losing the tariff-binding mechanism that produces Kirkerud's headline result of $2-17\%$ electric-boiler share variation across tariff structures. The defended simplification turns the LP-not-MILP choice into a precedent-anchored scope decision rather than an unmotivated assertion.

The objective function minimizes net electricity cost over the horizon:
\begin{align*}
\min \quad \sum_{t=1}^{8760} & \Big[ p^{\text{spot}}_t \cdot e^{\text{buy}}_t \Big] + \tau^{\text{grid}} \cdot \sum_{t} e^{\text{buy}}_t + \tau^{\text{elavg}} \cdot \sum_{t} e^{\text{buy}}_t \\
& - \sum_{t} p^{\text{spot}}_t \cdot e^{\text{sell}}_t - \pi^{\text{mFRR,cap}} \cdot K^{\text{mFRR}} - \sum_{t} \pi^{\text{mFRR,act}}_t \cdot a^{\text{mFRR}}_t
\end{align*}
where $p^{\text{spot}}_t$ is the hourly NO1 day-ahead price, $\tau^{\text{grid}}$ is the grid-tariff term of the baseline regime, the effective volumetric charge of approximately 5 øre/kWh the operator faces on its interruptible connection terms, $\tau^{\text{elavg}}$ is the reduced elavgift rate for district heating producers, $K^{\text{mFRR}}$ is committed mFRR capacity, and $\pi^{\text{mFRR,cap}}$ and $\pi^{\text{mFRR,act}}_t$ are mFRR capacity and activation prices. In the standard-tariff counterfactual the volumetric grid term is replaced by the effektledd term $T^{\text{eff}} \cdot \max_{m} P^{\text{peak}}_m$, the monthly-peak charge of Elvia's standard schedule; The monthly-peak term $\max_{m} P^{\text{peak}}_m$ matches how Elvia settles the capacity charge for Celsio's tariff class, on the single highest hourly draw of each calendar month rather than on an average of several peaks \cite{elvia_dagens_2022}. The three-peak-average settlement, the mean of the three highest daily maxima on separate days, applies to the trinn tariff for customers below 100{,}000 kWh per year and therefore not to Celsio; the regulator permits either basis, and the large-customer schedules use the single-peak form \cite{ove_flataker_national_2020}, \cite{langset_national_2021}, \utsett{(RME fact sheets - RME - 2020/2022)}. The single-peak encoding is thus the faithful settlement rule for the case, and it makes the effektledd maximally sensitive to a single load-raising hour.

Decision variables are hourly: electric-boiler heat dispatch, heat-pump heat dispatch, accumulator charge and discharge, electricity purchase from grid, mFRR commitment and activation. Heat demand is treated as inelastic, consistent with the scope decision in \autoref{1-subsec:1.4 scope and limitations} that excludes demand-side response from the analysis.

Constraints close the formulation along five axes. The heat-demand balance equates total heat output (waste-to-energy base-load plus heat-pump and electric-boiler dispatch plus accumulator discharge minus accumulator charge) to the exogenous hourly demand profile. The accumulator state-of-charge recursion bounds the storage trajectory by capacity, charge and discharge rates, and an initial-equal-terminal SOC constraint to prevent end-of-year exploitation. Capacity bounds apply per asset and at aggregate level. The mFRR participation constraint is two-sided. Committed capacity must clear the Statnett minimum-bid floor, which stood at 10 MW in NO2, NO4 and NO5 and 5 MW in NO1 and NO3 through the 2024 primary-scenario year \cite{dalen_fleksibilitet_nodate} and was cut to 1 MW with effect from 2 December 2025, after the mFRR Energy Activation Market had gone live on 4 March 2025 \cite{solberg_manedsrapport_nodate}\avklar{primary for the 2 December 2025 cut: Statnett news "1 MW som minste budstørrelse for mFRR-reserver fra 2. desember"} the post-Norgespris mini-scenario therefore applies the 1 MW floor. Committed capacity is additionally capped from above, $K^{\text{mFRR}} \leq K^{\text{eligible}}$, where $K^{\text{eligible}}$ takes the three access-axis levels of 29, 33, and 200 MW defined in the scenario stack; the ceiling encodes the capacity-area regime described in \autoref{2-subsec:2.3.2 market barriers} \cite{statnett_innforing_2026}. Ramp rates per technology bound hour-to-hour dispatch changes within physically defensible bounds; the relevant order of magnitude is anchored by the Sdr. Felding pilot reported in IEA HPT Annex 57, where a $1.3 MW$ heat pump records  7 minutes start-up and  4 minutes turn-down, placing heat-pump participation in the aFRR window while electric boilers retain near-instant FCR-range ramping \cite{v_pedersen_annex_2024}.

Outputs are annual operator cashflow and levelized cost of heat (NOK/MWh) under a scenario stack with two design axes on top of the representative-year split in \autoref{3-subsec:3.3.2 input data sources}. The tariff axis runs the vilkår baseline, the effective volumetric charge the operator actually faces, against a standard-tariff counterfactual that reinstates the monthly-peak effektledd term; the difference between the two prices the interruptible arrangement itself. The access axis runs the mFRR eligibility ceiling at 29 MW (today's connected reserve capacity), 33 MW (the published ceiling for the Oslo and Akershus capacity-area group \cite{statnett_innforing_2026}), and 200 MW and above (the counterfactual in which the operator's pending applications are approved); the three levels bracket the value of regulatory access. A no-flexibility dispatch, with the accumulator constrained to zero throughput and no spot-arbitrage or mFRR participation, remains the counterfactual against which operator flexibility value is measured. The recommended core is seven runs out of the twelve-cell full cross; \autoref{tab:3.3.1 scenario stack} states the selection and what each cell answers.

\subsubsection{Input Data and Sources}
\label{3-subsec:3.3.2 input data sources}

Model inputs are cataloged in \autoref{tab:3.3.2 model input}, with source, time period, and resolution. The thesis uses a two-scenario representative-year structure rather than a single year. The primary scenario uses the most recent complete calendar year that pre-dates the Norgespris extension to district heating customers (effective 1 October 2025), which is 2024, with 2023 as a fallback if the 2024 mFRR series is incomplete at submission. The primary scenario gives the cleanest data envelope: a full year of Nord Pool day-ahead prices, mFRR capacity and activation series from ENTSO-E Transparency and Nord Pool, and the Elvia effektledd schedule aligned under one regulatory regime, with no mid-year regime split that would distort the LP's revenue calculations. A secondary mini-scenario re-runs the dispatch under the post-October 2025 regime — Norgespris price ceiling indexed to a politically set retail electricity price, state-funded strømstøtte replacing operator self-financing, energiloven §5-5 ceiling still in force — using the most recent quarter or two of post-regime-change data available at submission. The two-scenario structure addresses the "data completeness versus contemporary regulatory regime" trade-off explicitly: the primary scenario carries the methodological weight for the BØ baseline and the gap arithmetic in \autoref{4-subsec:4.1 DH operator results}, and the mini-scenario quantifies how the post-Norgespris regulatory regime shifts the operator's flexibility-capture envelope under the most current "now" conditions. 

The 2024 mFRR data availability is confirmed: the capacity-price series is on file complete for 2019-2026 from the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform, with one missing source day (23 May 2024) zero-filled and documented, and the activation and day-ahead series are complete from Nord Pool, so 2024 stands as the primary year without recourse to the 2023 fallback. The post-Norgespris mini-scenario dates are fixed to Q4 2025 plus Q1 2026; every collected series - prices, reserves, exchange rates, and temperature - covers that window in full.

Spot-price data are sourced from Nord Pool's data portal at the day-ahead resolution Statnett's market-coupling settles, supplemented from ENTSO-E Transparency where historical depth is needed. mFRR capacity prices come from the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform's contracted-balancing-reserves series (17.1.B\&C), which republishes Statnett's procurement data \cite{entsoe_entsoe_nodate}, and mFRR activation prices from Nord Pool's balancing-market series \cite{nord_pool_nord_nodate}; the product distinction matters — the thesis models mFRR, not FCR-N or aFRR, and the THEMA-anchored FCR-N reserve value (NOK 0.33 m/MW/yr) cited in \autoref{3-subsec:3.3.3 assumptions} is a benchmark for revenue magnitude, not a substitute for mFRR-specific data.

The effektledd schedule is taken from Elvia's published tariff document for the relevant rate year; the parametrization enters the LP as the monthly-peak charge applied to the maximum hourly grid draw within each calendar month. A useful cross-check comes from the Norwegian tariff parameters reported in \cite{bergaentzle_cross-sector_2022}, Table 1: 1,307 €/MW capacity charge, 3 / 2 €/MWh volumetric, and 11,984 €/MW subscription. These figures are aggregated at transmission level and should not displace the Elvia schedule as primary calibration source - Celsio operates at distribution voltage, where the standard effektledd is more punitive, though the incumbent's interruptible terms reduce the effective burden to a volumetric charge - Connection voltage is the open per-site variable; the working assumption is that Celsio connects mainly around 33 kV, which places it in the regional grid, since ordinary distribution ends at 22 kV and Elvia's regionalnett has only two tiers, Nettnivå 1 at 132/66/48 kV and Nettnivå 2 at 22/11 kV, with no 33 kV level \cite{elvia_dagens_2022}. On both the over-100{,}000 kWh distribution effekttariff and the regionalnett business tariff, the capacity charge settles on the single highest hourly draw per calendar month; only a customer with its own settlement area (eget nettavregningsområde) is charged on transmission peak-load reference hours. The regionalnett capacity rate is roughly half the distribution high-voltage rate, about 34 against 68 NOK/kW per winter month in 2024, and its energy term is loss-based and capped rather than flat \cite{elvia_dagens_2022}, so the connection level materially shifts the standard-tariff counterfactual and is carried as a sensitivity.

The reduced electricity tax (elavgift) rate for district heating producers is 0.60 øre/kWh in both 2025 and 2026, with the standard rate at 7.13 øre/kWh in 2026; the rate is set in the annual Stortingsvedtak and published on Skatteetaten's rate page, while eligibility for the reduced rate is entity-level, following the tax regulation's test of a fuel mix at least half waste, bio, surplus heat and heat pumps, assessed company-wide per calendar year \cite{the_norwegian_tax_administration_arsrundskriv_nodate}\avklar{add the Stortingsvedtak om særavgifter / Skatteetaten rate page as its own source entry for the rate — rates are not printed in the årsrundskriv (2021 and later editions)}. Electric boilers are not a qualifying source, so a sustained large-scale power-to-heat share could in principle push the mix below the threshold and trigger the standard rate; the model's flat $\tau^{\text{elavg}}$ therefore holds only inside the qualifying-mix region, a boundary the operator sits far from. The $CO_2$ levy on waste incineration is, for 2025, NOK 908 / tonne for non-quota-liable emissions and NOK 182 / tonne for quota-liable emissions, falling to NOK 848 / tonne (non-quota) with an ETS exemption in 2026 \cite{stortinget_avfallsforbrenning_2025}; the earlier NOK 830 figure was the 2024 rate, and the applicable Klemetsrud rate depends on the plant's quota status in the representative year.

Hafslund Celsio asset capacities are taken from the audited 2025 segment data now on file — Hafslund's Foreløpig årsregnskap 2025 and Årsrapport konsernregnskap 2025 — supplemented by operator confirmation where granted. The 2025 audited accounts also confirm the Norgespris-to-DH effective date of 1 October 2025 and the accumulator-tank use for spot arbitrage, both of which calibrate the BØ baseline and serve as sanity checks on the LP output. The capacity structure is handled in \autoref{3-subsec:3.3.3 assumptions} as three tiers, each capping a different revenue stream: installed electric capacity of approximately 278 MW across 19 boilers (operator-stated, consistent with THEMA's 283 MW headline), concurrently-dispatchable capacity (an open operator data request), and reserve-eligible capacity of approximately 29 MW today against the published 33 MW ceiling for the Oslo and Akershus capacity-area group \cite{statnett_innforing_2026}.

The heat-demand profile is the most consequential operational input. Hafslund Celsio operational data at hourly resolution is the preferred source if access is granted under any agreed terms. \subsubsection{Assumptions and Simplifications}
\label{3-subsec:3.3.3 assumptions}

The LP carries eight consequential assumptions. Each is stated with the precedent that defends it and, where relevant, the alternative explicitly considered and not pursued. Per the methodology framework, simplification is the point: over-tooling was the failure mode of the prior thesis attempt, and the model's job is to size the gap, not to be the central output.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Assumption 1 — Perfect foresight on prices and demand.} The LP knows the full hour-by-hour spot, mFRR, and demand profile in advance and dispatches accordingly. The richer alternative is multi-stage stochastic programming, exemplified by \cite{ottesen_multi_2018}, who applied a stochastic MIP to aggregator bidding across RKOM, Elspot, and RK. Two report-grounded points strengthen the rejection. That analysis finds the perfect-information bound is $33\%$ above the stochastic solution, and that $84\%$ of that gap sits in approximately five unforecastable extreme-price days in January 2016, so stochastic machinery does not proportionally add value for the thesis's gap-quantification question. It also explicitly excludes grid tariffs and capacity charges (§4.6, p.130), which is precisely the effektledd the thesis places at the centre of the BØ constraint. The stochastic precedent is therefore the alternative that deliberately omits the tariff this thesis foregrounds. The operator's own planning practice corroborates the deterministic frame: dispatch is re-optimized hourly on a rolling horizon of roughly one week, deterministically and asset-backed (operator interview, Hafslund Celsio, 25 June 2026).

    \item \textbf{Assumption 2 — Fixed heat demand profile (no demand-side elasticity).} Heat demand enters the LP as an exogenous hourly series. The scope decision in Section 1.4 excludes end-user elasticity from the analysis; the thesis values flexibility from the operator-supply side, not from demand-side response.

    \item \textbf{Assumption 3 — Three-tier capacity structure.} Fleet capacity enters the model as three tiers, each capping a different revenue stream. Installed electric capacity of approximately 278 MW across 19 boilers, operator-stated and consistent with the 283 MW headline in THEMA (2024) §3.3, bounds spot arbitrage \avklar{confirm: ~278 MW / 19 boilers; per-site electric split owed by the operator}. Concurrently-dispatchable capacity, set by grid-connection and interruptible-terms limits, bounds what can run in any single hour; it is an open operator data request, pending which the installed tier is used with a stated caveat. Reserve-eligible capacity bounds mFRR revenue: approximately 29 MW is connected today (operator interview, Hafslund Celsio, 25 June 2026), against a published ceiling of 33 MW for the Oslo and Akershus capacity-area group \cite{statnett_innforing_2026}.. The asymmetry between the arbitrage-capable and reserve-eligible tiers, roughly 278 versus 29 to 33 MW, is a designed headline result of the scenario stack in \autoref{3-subsec:3.3.1 model formulation}, not an assumption footnote. The earlier conservative per-site sum of approximately 131 to 137 MW is superseded.

    \item \textbf{Assumption 4 — No capital-cost amortisation in the operational model.} The LP minimises operational cost only. Capex anchors (heat-pump 300–600 €/kW from David (2017); electric-boiler  0.04 M€/MW from Meibom (2007) with modern cross-reference  0.14 M€/MW from Trømborg (2017); accumulator 0.96 M€/2,000 m\textsuperscript{3} from Trømborg (2017)) are surfaced separately in Section 4.1 where relevant to the discussion, not amortised inside the dispatch objective.

    \item \textbf{Assumption 5 — mFRR only.} The model represents mFRR (manual frequency restoration reserve, 15-minute activation) but not aFRR (automatic frequency restoration reserve, 5-minute activation) or FCR (frequency containment reserve, sub-second). The aFRR capital-cost estimation is out of scope per Section 1.4 and flagged for future-work treatment in Section 5.5. The THEMA-anchored FCR-N value of approximately NOK 0.33 m/MW/yr serves as a magnitude benchmark in Section 3.3.2, with the product distinction kept explicit. The operator has bid mFRR since 2024; whether FCR or aFRR participation is worth pursuing is an open operator question (operator interview, Hafslund Celsio, 25 June 2026). Reserve commitment is in any case bounded by the access ceiling of \autoref{3-subsec:3.3.1 model formulation}.

    \item \textbf{Assumption 6 — Two representative-year scenarios (pre-Norgespris primary + post-Norgespris mini).} The model runs a primary scenario on a single calendar year of hourly data pre-dating the 1 October 2025 Norgespris extension to district heating (likely 2024, with 2023 as fallback), giving the cleanest single-regime data envelope. A secondary mini-scenario re-runs dispatch under the post-October 2025 regime to surface how Norgespris and the state-funded strømstøtte shift the operator's flexibility-capture envelope under the most current "now" conditions. Multi-year stochastic scenarios remain out of scope per Section 1.4. The two-scenario structure is the minimum needed to address both data completeness and contemporary regulatory regime. The 2024 primary year carries a warm-year caveat: 2024 was the third-warmest year on record, about +1.1 °C against normal, while 2023 was near normal, Measured degree-day data sharpen the caveat: heating degree days at Oslo–Blindern (base 17 °C) total 3,518 in 2024 against 3,761 in 2023, about 6.5\% fewer; the larger degree-day delta is consistent with the smaller volume delta once the weather-independent base load is netted out \cite{norsk_klima_service_senter_observasjoner_nodate}.

    \item \textbf{Assumption 7 — No network thermal dynamics.} The model abstracts over thermal-network propagation delays, temperature regimes, and pipeline losses. The alternative architecture, which incorporates network thermal dynamics for flexibility unlocking, is set out in \cite{vandermeulen_controlling_2018}. The thesis treats the network as a transport layer that does not bind the operator's flexibility envelope at the temporal resolution modelled. The static network-storage stock quantified in the Section 3.2 envelope is unaffected by this assumption: A7 excludes network thermal \emph{dynamics} from the dispatch optimisation, whereas the envelope reports a stock estimate that requires no propagation modelling.

    \item \textbf{Assumption 8 — Deterministic LP, not MILP with unit commitment.} The closest precedent includes unit commitment with binary start-up variables and biomass minimum-load constraints \cite{kirkerud_impacts_2016}. The thesis drops the unit-commitment binaries because Celsio's flexible assets — electric boiler, heat pump, accumulator — have no binding start-up or minimum-load constraints at the operator scale relevant to the gap analysis. The result is a precedent-anchored simplification rather than a bare assertion: the LP retains Kirkerud's deterministic perfect-foresight hourly-annual dispatch frame and drops the binaries the question does not need.
\end{itemize}

The bi-level strategic-investment alternative is explicitly rejected on scope grounds at the model-design level rather than the assumption level. A bi-level strategic-investment model with a price-maker assumption and KKT reformulation, on a stylized 6-node DH case, is the alternative considered here \cite{tan_strategic_2022}. The price-maker assumption does not transfer to the Norwegian operator setting, since no real Norwegian DH operator has market power at this scale. One public validation anchor closes the model design. Company-level statistics derived from SSB report Hafslund Celsio's 2025 net heat production at 1.8 TWh with a flexible-electricity share of 17\%, roughly 0.31 TWh of electric-boiler heat \cite{norsk_fjernvarme_fjernkontrollenno_nodate}, \cite{norsk_fjernvarme_fakta_nodate}. The modelled electric-boiler generation in the baseline run is checked against this observed share; the check requires no operator data, so it survives even if operator data access is refused.

\subsection{Socio-Economic Valuation}
\label{3-subsec:3.4 socio-economic valuation}

The socio-economic valuation triangulates published societal-value estimates against the THEMA (2024) anchor, isolating the flexibility-attributable component from THEMA's broader conversion-value headline. It does not construct a cost-benefit analysis from scratch. The framework choice, the anchor figure, the triangulation set, the outputs, the sensitivity logic, and the externality-coverage checklist are stated in this section; the numerical result and uncertainty range appear in Section 4.2, and the gap arithmetic and barrier attribution in Section 4.3.

The framework draws conceptually on the standard Norwegian framework for socio-economic analysis of public investments. The 25-year lifetime and 6\% real pre-tax discount rate adopted here are the parameters of NVE's alternative-analysis spreadsheet for district-heating concession applications, which \cite{Thema_fjernvarme_2024} reports, and criticizes, in its review of the concession procedure; the thesis adopts the same horizon and discount rate so the triangulation across the source set is commensurable, while noting that THEMA's own value estimates are stated as annual figures rather than as net present values. The choice to triangulate rather than construct a from-scratch CBA is warranted at corpus scale by \cite{ahmed_district_2025}: 123 studies reviewed, 19 of which monetized at least one DH benefit, only 10 of which described the monetization method, and 26 monetization instances across six methods (NPV, avoided cost, LCOH, NPC, hedonic pricing, willingness-to-pay). Flexibility as a benefit category was identified eleven times, quantified four times, and monetized zero times. The corpus-wide absence of a harmonized method is not a problem the thesis can solve by importing an off-the-shelf framework; triangulation across independent published estimates with explicit uncertainty is the field-appropriate response.

The anchor estimate is THEMA (2024) at NOK 1–1.6 bn/year. The figure requires a precise statement of what it measures. THEMA's headline is the socio-economic value of conversion — switching 1.0–1.5 TWh of electric heating to DH-served hydronic load — not the socio-economic value of thermal-flexibility dispatch. The composition is freed grid capacity at NOK 250–430 m/yr plus a gross freed-electricity value of NOK 750–1,200 m/yr, the latter being a fuel-switch and peak-reduction benefit reported gross of conversion and production costs. The flexibility-attributable subset is the reserve-market value (approximately NOK 0.33 m/MW/yr FCR-N on the 283 MW boiler fleet, an eligibility assumption that the capacity-area regime of \autoref{2-subsec:2.3.2 market barriers} caps in practice at roughly 29 to 33 MW) plus the storage- and optimization-enabled peak-capacity relief, not the freed-electricity conversion term. The thesis isolates the flexibility-attributable component and adopts THEMA's headline only as the upper bound on the broader conversion-plus-flexibility envelope, never as the value of flexibility itself.

The sponsor bias on the anchor estimate is acknowledged once here. THEMA (2024) was commissioned by Hafslund Celsio. The mitigation is triangulation across sources independent of the operator: NVE LA23 (2023) and NVE (2024) Utviklingen i kraftmarkedet mot 2050 supply the public-sector long-term market modeling \cite{nve_utviklingen_2024}; \cite{rosnes_energifleksible_2023} supplies the building-stock flexibility valuation; the Furuset neighbourhood case study \cite{kauko_assessing_2022} supplies the seasonal borehole-storage benefit analysis, and SINTEF ZEN Report 47 \cite{SINTEF_energy_2023} supplies the national building-stock peak-power and system-cost scenarios; the stochastic TIMES-Norway national DR magnitude and the hydropower-income-erosion distributional finding come from \cite{ahang_analysis_2025}; Grønli / AFRY (2025) supplies plant-level NOK/MW figures 
\avklar{VERIFY: Grønli / AFRY (2025) is operator-adjacent grey literature, not peer-reviewed — flag once and do not treat as a primary anchor}. 

Corroboration independent of Hafslund-commissioning comes from \cite{energikommisjonen_mer_2023} on national DH status, and the THEMA \& Multiconsult report for OED (2022) \cite{thema_consulting_group_har_2022}, which is independent of the Hafslund-commissioned THEMA 2024 and supplies the 1,000–5,000 MW capacity-balance need plus the anti-cyclical-availability framing relevant to Section 2.4. The output of the triangulation is a central socio-economic estimate expressed both as a unit value (NOK/MWh-flex) and as an annualised total (NOK/year), normalised against the technical flexibility envelope from Section 3.2 so the socio-economic and business-economic results share the same physical denominator, together with an externality-coverage table that documents which externalities are quantified, which are qualitatively acknowledged, and which are out of scope. Sensitivity analysis varies four parameters that anchor the most material SØ-side uncertainty. Carbon price is the swing variable identified across the corpus. A business-economic amortization result has been reported that swings between 15 years and infinity on small changes in electricity, gas, and $CO_2$ prices \cite{moser_socioeconomic_2018}. \avklar{the Moser price-sensitivity result sits in that study's business-economic layer (Section 5.3.5, the heat-merit-order investor case), so it warrants carbon-price sensitivity on the BØ side directly and on the SØ side only by extension. The sentence that follows still asserts that price and externality assumptions dominate the SØ result --- attach an SØ-side warrant for that claim, the THEMA carbon-price sensitivity being the obvious candidate, or soften it.} Price and externality assumptions therefore dominate the SØ result, and the thesis sensitivity logic is built around carbon-price variation rather than around technical-parameter perturbations. The other three varied parameters are the grid-investment unit cost (anchoring the avoided-grid-capacity component), the peak-load valuation (anchoring the freed-capacity component), and the hydropower-export value (anchoring the Norway-specific externality discussed below).

The methodological precedent for paired BØ + SØ analysis on a single DH asset is \cite{ostergaard_business_2019}. That pairing uses energyPRO for the BØ plant dispatch and EnergyPLAN for the SØ system valuation, neither of which is a custom-coded LP. It is therefore a precedent for the paired \emph{structure}, not for the thesis's LP method. The headline gap is directly on-point: BØ-optimal storage for the Ballen-Brundby plant settles at approximately 10–15 MWh, which the authors gloss as storage being "only relevant up to a size of 24 h of production", while the island-level EnergyPLAN analysis shows that far larger storage - the sweep runs to 3,200 MWh across Samsø's four DH plants — keeps reducing electricity export, at linearly rising total system cost and with diminishing returns. The authors identify no socioeconomic optimum; they conclude only that "there is a misalignment between what is relevant from a societal perspective and from a plant operator perspective". The two figures are not measured on the same system - 10–15 MWh is the 1.6 MW Ballen-Brundby plant in energyPRO, 3,200 MWh is the 6.3 MW / 25.5 GWh/year island DH system in EnergyPLAN. The mechanism transfers to the Norwegian operator case; the magnitudes do not. A second paired precedent combines a heat-merit-order BØ investment case with a SWOT and MOVE2social macroeconomic SØ \cite{moser_socioeconomic_2018}, though its SØ side is not a DFØ-style welfare CBA, so the Moser €44m GDP and €15m/yr export figures are not analogues to THEMA's welfare estimate. The novelty claim sits not on "first paired study" but on the paired BØ+SØ analysis applied to a Norwegian firm-level case with explicit gap quantification - the cell the literature review has left empty \autoref{2-subsec:2.6 literature review}.

The externality-coverage checklist completes the framework. Carbon-dioxide emissions are quantified through the THEMA anchor sensitivity and the Moser carbon-price sensitivity, with the Norwegian waste-incineration $CO_2$ levy (NOK 908 / tonne non-quota in 2025; NOK 182 / tonne quota) entering as a dispatch-cost parameter on Celsio's WtE baseload. Avoided peak and capacity investment in the power grid (\emph{nettnytte}) is quantified through the THEMA freed-grid-capacity figure alone; \cite{sandberg_energy_2019} corroborates only the upstream step — that grid tariff structure significantly influences flexible electricity use in Nordic district heating, with load demand tariffs based on maximum monthly load demand hampering it — and does not itself value avoided grid capacity. Avoided renewable curtailment is qualitatively acknowledged because Norway is hydropower-dominated and structural surplus rarely takes the form Danish-style P2H absorbs (Section 2.4.3). Security of supply is qualitatively acknowledged but not separately quantified. Air quality is out of scope. The Norway-specific externality of freed hydropower export and balancing capacity follows \cite{askeland_balancing_2019}: DH flexibility frees hydropower for export and balancing rather than absorbing wind curtailment. The Askeland mechanism is acknowledged qualitatively in the central estimate but not separately quantified; the scope guardrail rules out building a methodology around the hydropower-export framing.

\subsection{Qualitative Interviews}

The qualitative component triangulates the mechanism attribution that the quantitative analysis cannot validate from the dispatch numbers alone. \subsubsection{Sampling and Recruitment}
\label{3-subsec:3.5.1 sampling}

Sampling is purposive across the four institutional positions that together constitute the BØ/SØ gap mechanism. Each actor is interviewed for the perspective they uniquely have, not as a representative of a population. Saturation is not the relevant criterion for purposive expert sampling at this scale; the design pursues analytical reach across institutional positions rather than statistical reach within any one.

The exclusions are documented in Section 2 of the qualitative methods guide and surfaced here so the four-role logic is visible and defensible.

The sample size is four interviews, one per role. Defended on purposive-sampling grounds rather than saturation, adding more interviews within one role would deepen that perspective without strengthening the four-role mechanism logic. If recruitment fails for one role, the gap is stated explicitly in the limitations section and no weaker substitute is accepted — substituting NVE for RME, or Elvia for Statnett, would introduce evidentiary noise rather than reduce the limitation. The latter reports that actors without a background in energy, such as property owners, were less familiar with energy concepts than energy actors, and also found it harder to price their resources, lacking experience in pricing electricity usage — a knowledge asymmetry that is one reason the operator-side framing of this thesis puts the DH utility, not its connected buildings, at the centre of the flexibility question.

\subsubsection{Interview Protocol}
\label{3-subsec:3.5.2 interview protocol}

Interviews are semi-structured, approximately 30–45 minutes, conducted in Norwegian. The thematic guide \utsett{ref intervjuguide (appendix?)} and is reproduced in full in the appendix) covers six themes without imposing a fixed question order. The themes are background and organizational context; current flexibility practice; technical possibilities and limitations; business-economic incentives and barriers; socio-economic perspective and system value (the directly gap-relevant theme); and frameworks, regulation, and future outlook.

Role-specific emphasis weighting applies the same base guide differently across the four actors. The Hafslund Celsio interview weights Themes 2–4, with specific probes on effektledd's impact on electric-boiler dispatch, the real reasons not to bid into mFRR, and the dispatch-logic effect of the waste-incineration $CO_2$ levy (NOK 908 / tonne non-quota in 2025). The RME interview weights Themes 5–6, with specific probes on the response to THEMA (2024) recommendations, the status of any effektledd review (the regulator's own 2020 tariff-reform consultation document supplies an anchor here \cite{eriksen_proposed_2022}), and the view on Nordic comparative results \cite{bergaentzle_cross-sector_2022}, \cite{sandberg_energy_2019}. The Statnett interview weights Themes 3 and 6, with specific probes on the capacity-area rationale and cap values, the contradiction between inviting 1 MW entry and capping per-area allocation, deliverability assessment without a sub-110 kV physical layer, internal alignment (the operator reports receiving differing answers depending on who is asked, an expectation carried into the analysis as a probe and an analysis note), openness to DH-tailored aggregation rules, and awareness of the "DH as virtual storage" framing - \cite{mitridati_heat_2020} for the institutional gap and \cite{mitridati_power_2018} for the formal storage equivalence. The Energidepartementet interview weights Theme 6, with specific probes on how Aasland's E24 (2025-09-05) statements translate into concrete instruments, the status of cross-ministry coordination, and the view on the THEMA NOK 1–1.6 bn estimate (with the conversion-vs-flexibility distinction surfaced for the ministerial discussion).

Three Norwegian-specific topical anchors recur across interviews where relevant. The waste-incineration $CO_2$ levy is a Norway-specific policy distortion not present in the Danish or Swedish corpus and the dispatch-logic implications matter for the operator interview most directly. The Askeland hydropower-export framing — that DH flexibility frees hydropower for export and balancing rather than absorbing wind curtailment — is particularly relevant for Statnett and the ministry, where the macroeconomic and balancing-market mechanisms intersect. The "missing flexibility product" framing — that DH functions as virtual electricity storage without a contract type to sell that flexibility into — is particularly relevant for RME and Statnett, where the regulatory and market-design responses live.

Consent and recording follow NTNU and SIKT requirements. Written consent forms are issued before the interview. Audio recording requires explicit consent. In-person interviews are recorded using the NTNU Nettskjema-diktafon-app, which uploads directly to NTNU infrastructure; digital interviews use the built-in recording in Teams or Zoom. No personal phone recording is used without immediate upload to NTNU storage. Recordings are stored separately from transcripts; recordings are deleted on thesis submission, transcripts are retained per the SIKT-approved retention period. Participants receive the specific quotes that will appear in the thesis (in context) for confirmation before submission — standard courtesy that reduces the risk of misinterpretation complaint.

\subsubsection{Analysis Approach}
\label{3-subsec:3.5.3 analysis approach}

The analytical method is reflexive thematic analysis, grounded epistemologically in \cite{cunliffe_crafting_2010}. That source is cited once for the interpretive (versus positivist) epistemological position: the researcher's embeddedness is part of the method, not a threat to it. The three-problematic framework it also offers is overkill for a support-level method component and is not imported.

Reflexive thematic analysis is preferred over three alternatives. Grounded theory is rejected because the thesis is theory-informed — the Sneum (2021) barrier taxonomy, the BØ/SØ framing, and the Mitridati virtual-storage concept all enter as a priori sensitising frames. RTA accommodates a priori theoretical sensitisation without forcing the analysis into a fully inductive coding scheme. Content analysis is rejected because the goal is interpretive depth on N=4, not frequency counting across a large corpus. Interpretative phenomenological analysis is rejected because the focus is institutional and professional perspectives, not lived experience.

The analysis follows the six-phase RTA process. Phase 1 is familiarisation with the transcripts and field notes; each transcript is read twice before coding anything, with a reflexive notebook of impressions kept alongside the data. Phase 2 is initial open coding, semantic and latent, capturing both the language actors use and the underlying assumptions; the coding tool is structured markdown, sufficient for N=4 (MAXQDA or NVivo would be appropriate at larger scale but are not required here). Phase 3 is constructing candidate themes, understood as patterns of shared meaning organised around a central concept rather than topic buckets. Phase 4 reviews the candidate themes against the full dataset and drops those that do not hold up. Phase 5 defines and names the surviving themes with one-sentence definitions that survive scrutiny. Phase 6 produces the report, with themes appearing in the Discussion chapter and each illustrated with one to three direct quotes — quotes are evidence, thematic synthesis is the argument.

Three a priori sensitising themes are declared upfront to maintain the theory-informed posture without foreclosing emergent material. Mechanism attribution asks which barriers actors actually experience as binding, against the Sneum (2021) taxonomy. Frame divergence asks how actors describe the value of flexibility — whether the operator's profit-within-rules frame and the regulator's system-needs frame produce different descriptions of the same mechanism. Policy openness asks which changes actors signal as live possibilities — whether THEMA-style critiques, mFRR minimum-bid redesigns, or sector-coupling instruments are framed as feasible or off the table. Emergent themes are allowed to compete with these — RTA is not a deductive coding scheme — and if material surfaces that the a priori frame does not capture, the emergent theme wins.

Integration with quantitative results runs in three priority moves. Confirmation or disconfirmation asks whether the operator interview confirms that effektledd is the binding constraint the LP model identifies as such — if yes, the Section 4.3 attribution strengthens; if no, the discrepancy surfaces in the discussion as a finding, not a problem. Mechanism enrichment asks how a mechanism matters operationally beyond that it matters: quote-level detail on how effektledd shapes day-to-day dispatch decisions enriches the gap-attribution narrative the LP can only size in aggregate. Surfacing missed mechanisms asks whether interviews reveal mechanisms the quantitative model does not capture — internal investment hurdles, reputational risk, supplier-contract terms — which are stated as qualitative additions to the gap attribution and flagged as out-of-scope for the LP model. Integration is not a positivist validation question; agreement is not the test, and the triangulation is about coverage and complementarity rather than mutual confirmation.

An optional theoretical lens is available \cite{salling_incentives_2024}: the framing that competition-based incentives can marginalise deeper energy-conservation logics is useful when interview analysis surfaces a divergence between operator and regulator framings. The lens turns that divergence from a methodological inconvenience into a substantive finding when it appears. That work uses ethnography and STS, not RTA, so the citation is for the theoretical concept only, not the method. The framing is used sparingly: themes emerge first, then the Salling lens is checked for whether it organises them productively. If interviews do not surface a competition-versus-conservation pattern, the reference is dropped.

The framing in the thesis methodology chapter remains Level 2 reinforcement: the model and valuation triangulation carry the argument; interviews contextualise and stress-test, they do not prove. Section 4.4 reports the RTA themes and quotes, Section 5.1 and 5.2 integrate the qualitative findings with the quantitative results, and the appendix carries the coding scheme and a sample of codes per theme.

\subsection{Methodological Limitations}
\label{3-subsec:3.7}

Four method-level limitations carry through the design — constraints baked into the chosen architecture rather than scope decisions about the substantive problem. The distinction matters: \autoref{5-subsec:5.5 future work} carries the study-level limitations and future-work directions that surface from running the analysis; this section names the constraints the design itself carries by construction. The treatment covers the most significant items rather than an exhaustive list, with each item announced, justified, and pointed forward to \autoref{5-subsec:5.5 future work}.

The first limitation is the single-case design and the generalisability question it raises. A single case examined in depth cannot generate statistical inferences about the population of Norwegian DH systems. The counter is that the design pursues analytical generalization to theory rather than statistical generalization to a population; the gap is a function of national regulatory context, which is uniform across Norwegian cases, and operator-specific cost structure, which is local to Oslo and Hafslund Celsio. Section 5.4 develops the conditional transferability of the Oslo findings to Trondheim, Bergen, and the smaller Nordic operators using the four regulatory criteria (revenue adequacy, economic efficiency, non-discrimination, simplicity and stability) of \cite{bergaentzle_electricity_2019} as the parallel scaffold introduced in Section 3.6. Nordic comparators \autoref{2-subsec:2.4.3 norwegian own case} strengthen the transferability claim without converting it into statistical generalization.

The second limitation is that the socio-economic estimate is triangulated rather than constructed from scratch. A systematic review of 123 studies found 26 monetization instances across six methods with no harmonized framework \cite{ahmed_district_2025}. Flexibility specifically was identified eleven times, quantified four times, and monetised zero times. The corpus has not converged on a method, which is the corpus-wide warrant for triangulating published estimates rather than building a bespoke CBA whose method choices would themselves be ungrounded. The mitigation is triangulation across sources independent of the Hafslund-commissioned THEMA anchor: NVE LA23, NVE (2024), Vista Analyse (2023), SINTEF ZEN Report 47, Grønli/AFRY (2025), NOU 2023:3, and the THEMA \& Multiconsult OED (2022) report. The sponsor-bias caveat on the THEMA (2024) anchor is acknowledged in Section 3.4 and not duplicated here. The sub-limitation worth surfacing — that THEMA's headline measures conversion value rather than flexibility dispatch value, so the method risk is mis-attributing the conversion benefit to flexibility — is addressed by the explicit isolation step in Section 3.4 that extracts the flexibility-attributable subset rather than adopting the headline. The forward-looking direction in Section 5.5 is the construction of a Norwegian DH-flexibility CBA framework that codifies the monetization method the corpus has not yet converged on.

The third limitation is perfect foresight and the choice of a deterministic LP. The model knows the full hour-by-hour spot, mFRR, and demand profile in advance, which overstates the operator's actual decision-making information by an unknowable margin. The counter has three parts. First, perfect-foresight deterministic dispatch is the standard simplification across the Nordic dispatch corpus: \cite{kirkerud_impacts_2016}, \cite{johannsen_incentivising_2021}, and \cite{tromborg_flexible_2017} all operate under this simplification, with the Kirkerud variant being MILP rather than LP, from which the thesis steps down \autoref{3-subsec:3.3.3 assumptions} A8 on operator-scale grounds. Second, the stochastic alternative \cite{ottesen_multi_2018} was considered and rejected on scope grounds. That analysis reports a $33\%$ perfect-information-versus-stochastic gap concentrated in approximately five unforeseeable extreme-price days in January, so the stochastic machinery does not proportionally serve the gap-quantification aim. It also explicitly excludes the grid tariffs and capacity charges the thesis places at the centre of the BØ constraint, so the stochastic precedent is the alternative that brackets the exact effektledd the thesis foregrounds. Third, the bi-level strategic-investment alternative \cite{tan_strategic_2022} is rejected because its price-maker assumption does not transfer to the Norwegian operator setting. The forward-looking direction in \autoref{5-subsec:5.5 future work} is a stochastic extension of the LP, particularly under high-price-volatility scenarios where the perfect-information assumption is most strained.

The fourth limitation is the first-order character of the technical flexibility envelope. The envelope in \autoref{3-subsec:3.2 analytical approach} is a static stock-and-headroom calculation whose result scales near-linearly with its two least certain inputs, the network water volume and the admissible temperature band, and it brackets the network thermal dynamics excluded by assumption A7. The counter is that the envelope's job is to bound, not to resolve: it supplies the upper bound and the common physical denominator for the gap arithmetic, and the sensitivity of that bound to the temperature-band assumption is reported alongside the estimate in Chapter 4. Where operator-confirmed values from Hafslund Celsio replace the engineering estimates \autoref{3-subsec:3.3.2 input data sources}, the envelope tightens accordingly. One input-side caveat spans the catalogue-sourced parameters. Danish Energy Agency cost data for immature technologies carries real between-edition revision risk: the 2026 edition doubled the 20 MW seawater heat-pump capex and raised geothermal levelized heat costs by 14 to 18\% against the previous sheets, on the agency's own admission that the then-young technologies had unreliable data. The electric-boiler and tank figures used here are mature-technology entries (category 3 to 4) where the catalogue assesses uncertainty as low \cite{danish_energy_agency_technology_2024}.
